Bee Well: gamifying goals to improve wellness

A working prototype of a health management app that helps young adults build healthy habits and manage their goals, designed with my team at the University of Florida.

Role
Lead Designer · research & design
Timeline
January – April 2022
Tools
Figma, Miro, Adobe Photoshop
Team
Maclay Ramsey & Kenzie Reneemann (UI), Joseph Addison (Visual), Eric Linger (UX Research)
01

Overview

An Analysis of College Students' Nutrition Practices conducted in 2022 showed that most students struggle to develop healthy habits due to lack of time and availability. Studies suggest these habits persist into adulthood, increasing the risk of conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.

How can we motivate students to overcome barriers like a busy lifestyle and poor dietary information to build and maintain sustainable, healthy habits?

02

Pain points

Through a literature review and user interviews, we identified why young adults struggle to accomplish their goals.

01

Poor time management

78% of students say they struggle with time management throughout college.

02

Lack of motivation

"It's hard to prioritize my health goals when I'm frequently exhausted by my schedule."

03

Difficulty maintaining a routine

"My current health goal is trying to put myself on my own set schedule and give myself a sense of direction."

Solution

A mobile platform that uses gamification to encourage young adults to set realistic goals, build a community of like-minded people, and learn about healthy practices.

03

User research

We conducted five interviews with health-conscious people who value nutrition and want to improve their wellness but struggle to maintain healthy habits. Our goals were to:

  • Understand users' current struggles and interests regarding their health
  • Learn about their daily lives and how our product could address their problems
  • Analyze why users had met, or failed to meet, their goals in the past

We synthesized the findings into personas outlining pain points, goals and what had worked for them before.

K

Kevin

A sophomore who often procrastinates, putting off tedious tasks to focus on hobbies. He wants a fun way to motivate himself.

Pain point
Puts off "boring" tasks; life lacks structure
Goal
A routine he can enjoy and keep long-term
J

Jane

Very involved in clubs and works part-time. She often overbooks herself and wants a way to manage her schedule and reduce stress.

Pain point
Overwhelmed by her schedule
Goal
Reduce stress
S

Sam

Recently started a new job and found a routine that balances work and social life, but wants to add goals like exercising.

Pain point
Worried her routine can't adapt to new goals
Goal
Accountability across multiple tasks

What helped them before: the sense of accomplishment from crossing off tasks, calendar apps, and project management apps with clean, easy UI.

04

Our strategy

We used Miro to run the Disney Creative Thinking exercise, brainstorming the problems people face when trying to improve their health, why they fail, and possible solutions. We then organized these into an affinity diagram to find common themes.

Design features

01

Welcome survey

During onboarding, users outline their goals and challenges, and Bee Well personalizes the experience, for example by suggesting relevant articles.

02

Suggested articles

Articles based on users' interests, such as nutrition, exercise or tips to reduce stress.

03

Honeycombs

An in-app currency earned by completing goals, reading articles or competing with friends, and spent in the Store on accessories for your bee.

04

Goal tracking

Daily or long-term goals with customizable reminders.

05

Hive

A community of people with similar goals who share progress and join friendly competitions.

We then mapped a user flow in Miro to analyze how people would move through the product, considering multiple scenarios and the pages needed to avoid "dead ends" where users' needs went unmet.

05

Design & feedback

Our mid-fidelity prototype focused on interaction and basic structure, highlighting the bee theme with honeycomb motifs throughout.

What is the purpose of the honeycombs? Why should the user care about earning them?

Feedback

We had added the points system to encourage users to complete tasks, but hadn't considered how honeycombs actually benefit them. To give them a reason to care, we added an in-app Store where users could customize their bee by buying accessories.

Mid-fidelity prototype

Grayscale screens for onboarding, the welcome survey, articles, goals and the bee's inventory let us test structure and flow before adding color.

Iteration

Giving honeycombs a purpose

Feedback questioned why users should care about earning honeycombs. The Store and inventory let users spend them dressing up their bee, turning points into a reward.

06

Final design

Every feature traces back to what we heard.

The high-fidelity design brings in a teal, honey and orange palette with honeycomb motifs, and an illustrated bee whose mood reflects each user's progress.

Welcome survey

Personalized from the first screen

Feedback: Respondents had a range of pain points (stress, time management, physical health) and wanted personalized goal tracking.

Solution: An onboarding survey focused on each user's goals and challenges, generating tips and articles tailored to them.

Suggested articles

Learning how to succeed

Feedback: Users knew what hadn't worked before, but some didn't know how to succeed, struggling to start and stay consistent.

Solution: Articles and tips that educate users on their interests and the best ways to reach their goals.

Honeycombs

Motivation you can see

Feedback: Lack of motivation was a main barrier, especially for long-term goals.

Solution: Gamification through a points system, earned for meeting goals and for activities like reading articles. The home screen shows the bee leveling up alongside goals met today and this month.

Goal tracking

Routines that bend, not break

Feedback: College students needed a routine adaptable enough to keep for at least a semester.

Solution: Habits form through repetition, so users set realistic goals, customizing when and how often they act and when they get reminders.

Hive

Accountability with friends

Feedback: Users needed accountability and wanted a community with similar goals.

Solution: Add friends, join competitions, or simply have a companion while working toward goals.

07

Reflections

I'm proud of what my team achieved, and with the experience I've gained since, there are things I'd do differently.

Wireframes

Our wireframes were closer to a mid-fidelity prototype. Wireframes should focus on structure and layout so we can test the assumptions from research and make sure risky features and high-traffic areas work, without being distracted by aesthetics. In future projects, I build proper wireframes and get feedback before designing visual elements.

Design

  • Consider color contrast and WCAG guidelines when choosing the palette
  • Reduce the size of the profile icon
  • Add labels under icons in the navigation bar
  • Add more feedback through micro-interactions and states like hover and error
