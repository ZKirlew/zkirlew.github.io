MedPortal: from reactive chaos to proactive clarity

A high-fidelity medical-device customer portal that keeps the OR on schedule, with ordering, service and asset readiness unified in one source of truth.

Role
Solo Product Designer
Responsibilities
End-to-end design process
Timeline
May 2026
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Claude
01

Context

The people keeping OR rooms running had no real visibility into the tools they depended on.

Hospitals rely on medical device suppliers for critical equipment, consumables and maintenance, yet 73% of B2B buyers actively avoid suppliers over poor digital experience. Digital portals are often built around internal org charts rather than users' needs. As a result, users struggle to find the information they need and default to calling sales reps.

MedPortal is a high-fidelity concept that addresses the problems medical device companies face in their existing customer portals.

02

The problem

Three separate frustrations, all with the same root cause.

By analyzing call log data and interviewing professionals in the industry, I identified three main pain points.

01

The anxiety loop: "I don't know where my order is"

Affects every user type, every order, every day. It isn't an edge case; it's the central daily experience of using the portal.

02

The portal maze: "I don't know where to go"

The portal mirrored the vendor's internal structure, not the tasks users needed to complete. Users defaulted to calling a rep who knew where everything lived.

03

Reactive-only maintenance

Equipment would hit the PM schedule after a procedure was already affected. No predictive signals, no proactive scheduling, no early warning.

03

User personas

Different roles. Similar problems.

Each persona represented a distinct task orientation and a moment where the current portal let them down. Rather than designing one experience for "hospital staff," I designed role-based dashboards that surfaced exactly what each person needed the moment they logged in.

JT

John Thompson

Procurement Lead · 8 yrs experience

Manages 40+ active orders across 18 approved vendors. His day starts by checking what's delayed and ends by making sure OR rooms are stocked for the next morning's cases.

SC

Sarah Chen

OR Clinical Specialist · 5 yrs experience

Responsible for device readiness across 8 OR rooms. Needs to know which equipment is cleared for the day's first case and which isn't.

DK

David Kim

Biomedical Engineer · 12 yrs experience

Manages preventive maintenance for multiple devices across the hospital. Needs to stay ahead of PM schedules and catch at-risk devices before they become OR incidents.

Journey maps

One bad week, from three points of view

I mapped each persona's journey through a real failure scenario: their actions, pain points, touchpoints and emotional state at every stage, and where MedPortal steps in.

04

Key design decisions

Data-driven design choices.

Each decision traces back to something I heard from users. Every piece of feedback below directly shaped the design decision below it.

01
User feedback 01

"I only find out about delays when I call the rep. By then, it's already a problem for the OR schedule."

Procurement Lead
Led to
Design decision 01

A proactive notification badge on the delay event, not just the current status

Rather than showing only where the order is now, the timeline shows exactly when MedPortal notified the user and who else was looped in automatically. The delay badge reads: "You were notified 26 minutes after this was reported." That's the thing the old system never did.

02
User feedback 02

"The robotic system threw an error code during a procedure. We had no idea the calibration was 17 days overdue."

OR Clinical Specialist
Led to
Design decision 02

An AI risk score surfaced before the device is touched, not after it fails

The Device Detail page shows an AI-calculated risk score (87/100 = HIGH), three contributing factors, and a predicted failure window. Sarah sees this the moment she opens the device record, before the 10:30am case starts. The score is tied to real usage data, not a generic warning.

03
User feedback 03

"I have to go to three different sections just to see which devices are overdue and when I can schedule a PM."

Biomedical Engineer
Led to
Design decision 03

Task-based dashboards, not org-chart navigation

Each role gets a dashboard oriented around their primary job. David's Maintenance Hub combines the at-risk device queue with a 30-day Gantt PM calendar and an AI scheduling recommendation in a single view. No tab-switching, no hunting across sections built for a different team's workflow.

05

Competitive analysis

What gaps exist in the current market?

I researched six similar platforms in the healthcare space. Each does a good job of addressing the issues a specific type of user may encounter; however, none offers a unified digital experience.

No platform connects a delayed shipment to the device it serves, notifies all affected roles automatically, and surfaces that connection in a clinical-first UX.

Competitive takeaway

The strongest platform should act as a personalized dashboard, prioritizing proactive systems with a clinical-first UX.

06

Information architecture

Designs built on a strong foundation.

Based on the competitive research, I outlined a site map before moving on to visual design.

Design decision 01

Downstream-impact routing, not status broadcasting

Is there a case scheduled in the next 48 hours requiring this item?

If yes, the OR charge nurse and the surgeon's coordinator receive a calm, specific notification automatically, 26 minutes after detection.

Design decision 02

Role-aware home screen

During onboarding, users select their role and the system directs them to a personalized dashboard based on their needs.

Design decision 03

Proactive risk, not reactive recovery

Make a device failure feel like a scheduled service appointment, not a crisis.

The AI risk score synthesizes usage frequency, repeat fault codes, warranty status, and fleet-wide model patterns into a single number per device.

  • An amber indicator fires 7 days before the overdue threshold.
  • A red alert fires overnight if a device crosses the threshold.
07

Key design tension

Alert fatigue.

A proactive system helps address potential issues early, but if alerts are too frequent, users are trained to ignore them.

Solution

Alerts depend on urgency and required action

The system evaluates how quickly a response is needed and whether action is required. An alert only goes through if it scores high on both. Users can also update their preferences at any time in their settings.

08

Projected outcome

How success is measured across all three issues.

The projected impact of personalized dashboards and a proactive system, for the people using the portal and for the business behind it.
