All work Case study 05 · 3D · VR · 2022

CyberDream: 3D worlds for an immersive VR experience

Designed 3D assets for Emerge Home's interactive VR environments, balancing a handcrafted look with real-time performance.

Role
3D Environment Artist: modeling, texturing & Unity implementation
Timeline
4-month internship, Jan – Apr 2022
Tools
Maya, Photoshop, Substance Painter, Unity
Team
Lead & two Environment Artists, Concept Artist, two Producers, Level Designers
01

Objective

Emerge Home contracted CyberDream VR, a VR game studio, to develop games for a virtual social gathering space.

As a 3D Environment Artist, I modeled and textured 3D assets for a VR party game in Unity. I collaborated with the concept artist and other environment artists to keep my assets consistent with the design guidelines, technical constraints and client expectations.

02

Technical challenges

Problem 01 · Texture memory

Solution: trim sheets

Texture memory is a common constraint in game engines like Unity and Unreal. If textures aren't managed well, visuals load slowly or slow down the whole game, resulting in a poor player experience. I used trim sheets, which combine many textures into one instead of importing multiple textures into the engine.

Problem 02 · Baking

Solution: a separate lightmap UV set

To display lighting and effects properly, assets need to be "baked," but trim sheets can disrupt baking and cause textures to render incorrectly. I created two separate UV sets, one for the trim sheet and one for baking, and combined them in Unity to display both texture and lighting information correctly in-game.

03

Iterations

Feedback · Volcano silhouette

A more natural-looking shape

My lead felt the first pass of the volcano silhouette was too sharp and looked unnatural. I rounded out some edges and, after studying real-life references, broke the shape into smaller rock chunks.

Feedback · Lava flow & color

A dynamic lava flow

Testing in-engine showed we needed more color contrast for player readability, and the lava could look more realistic. We chose a dark gray for the final level, and I adjusted the lava to follow the volcano's natural curves.

04

Challenges & takeaways

I learned so much through this internship and am grateful for my lead's and coworkers' support.

01

Style guidelines

The client wanted visuals that looked handcrafted, like DIY projects. When texturing, I considered the materials someone might use to make each object and the kinds of mistakes they'd make. I learned not to get too attached to my ideas and to adapt to the project's needs.

02

Collaboration

Game development is collaborative; different disciplines work together to meet the client's and users' expectations. Understanding where designers sit in the development pipeline helped me prepare my work for seamless handoff.

03

Technical constraints

Every platform has benefits and limitations. As a designer, it's my job to make sure people can interact with a design as intended despite those limits, like using trim sheets to reduce texture memory and keep rendering correct.
